File 01

A funnel that outperformed a major agency — on a smaller budget.

Telehealth lead generation  ·  Marketing Manager

My manager wanted broad targeting to maximize traffic. Competitor research told a different story: our real differentiator was on-site, 1:1 provider care — something most telehealth competitors couldn't offer, at a premium price point.

I made the case, backed by data, that broad targeting was pulling in price-sensitive traffic that didn't fit a premium service. We shifted to targeting people willing to pay for hands-on care, and rebuilt the funnel around that insight.

The result was a four-stage funnel — eligibility quiz, objection-handling sales page, calendar booking, and automated nurture — designed so information unfolds step by step instead of overwhelming a visitor on one page.

File 02

Monitoring 450+ companies from a single spreadsheet.

Cross-border trade program  ·  Project Coordinator, KOTRA

I supported a government-backed program connecting Korean export companies with outside marketing agencies — the companies received subsidies specifically to use agency services, and I sat in the middle: managing reimbursement data and checking that agencies delivered what they promised.

Rather than checking every company by hand, I built a monitoring approach inside one shared spreadsheet — conditional formatting to auto-flag companies over 70% of their allocated budget, with remaining balances calculating automatically. Pivot tables and XLSTAT handled the broader spend trends.

I also helped draft quarterly reports on North American logistics trends, in Korean, for official publication — writing that had to hold up at a professional level, not just a conversational one.

File 03

Owning a content pipeline, end to end.

Social content & creator management  ·  Marketing Coordinator

At a medical group in Korea, I owned the TikTok and Instagram content pipeline — producing content in-house while also sourcing and managing partnerships with outside influencers and UGC creators, from brief through revisions to final approval.

Running in-house production and creator collaborations side by side gave a steady volume of content without approval delays, and real data on which format — ours or theirs — actually performed better.
